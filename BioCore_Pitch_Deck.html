01 / The problem

Access control is stuck between insecure and invasive.

Every option forces a bad trade — convenience, security, or privacy. You rarely get two, never all three.

Cards · PINs · registers

Easily defeated

Badges get lost, shared and cloned. PINs get passed around. Paper registers are slow and faked. None prove who walked in.

Legacy face systems

A privacy liability

They store raw biometrics, skip consent, can't erase, and mix every org's faces together. Under DPDP / GDPR, that's a lawsuit waiting.

All of the above

No real identity

Recognising "a face we've seen" isn't knowing who someone is. No government-grade proof sits behind the match.

02 / Why now

The rules just changed — and the tech is finally ready.

Regulation

Privacy law has teeth

India's DPDP Act, GDPR and their cousins now demand consent, minimisation and real erasure. Most biometric systems physically cannot comply.

Demand

Contactless is default

People expect to walk in — not tap, queue, or hand over a card. Events, campuses and workplaces want frictionless entry at scale.

Capability

Recognition matured

Modern embeddings + liveness are accurate and cheap. The bottleneck is no longer the match — it's trust, consent and identity.

04 / How it works

From invite to gate — one clean lifecycle.

1

Invite & consent

The person is invited, logs in, and gives purpose-specific consent — before anything is collected.

consent-before-collection · purpose-bound
2

Government verification

Identity is proven against an authorised source. Only minimal verified claims are kept — never the raw record or photo.

provider-agnostic KYC · data minimisation
3

Encrypted face credential

A fresh live capture becomes a template, encrypted with a per-tenant key and bound to that person, purpose and org.

AES-256-GCM · per-tenant KMS keys
4

Walk-up entry

At the gate the person shows their face. BioCore matches, checks liveness and policy, and allows or denies.

1:1 & 1:N · liveness · authorization plane
5

Lifecycle & erasure

Credentials expire on policy, revoke instantly, and erase on request with a verifiable certificate. Everything is audited.

retention · revocation · proof-of-erasure

07 / Security architecture

Defence in depth, from the template to the gate.

At rest

Envelope encryption

AES-256-GCM data keys wrapped by per-tenant KMS/HSM keys. Templates are context-bound — copy one to another tenant and it simply won't decrypt.

Key custody

Only services decrypt

A pluggable KMS holds keys out of the database. Software keys are dev-only and hard-blocked in production.

At the edge

Trusted terminals

Devices paired, certified, revocable; every gate request signed, timestamped and nonce-protected against replay.

Assurance

Tested like it matters

Spoof programs (print, replay, mask, injection) and a security suite (isolation, template-copy, replay, erasure) run continuously.
